### The bridge from Transformer to GPT

I read GPT-1 after BERT, even though it comes earlier chronologically. That order makes the split very clear: BERT uses the Transformer to build bidirectional representations, while GPT preserves the decoder’s causality and starts from a naturally generative objective. Every token can only observe the tokens before it, and the model learns by predicting the next one.

The paper’s thesis is simple but decisive: labeled data for individual tasks is scarce, while unlabeled text is abundant. Learning should therefore be divided into two stages. First, train a high-capacity language model on a general corpus; then adapt it through supervised fine-tuning to specific problems.

### What gets transferred

Pre-training does not directly teach sentiment analysis, entailment, or question answering. It teaches a language representation that captures structures and dependencies shared by those tasks. During fine-tuning, a linear head is added and the Transformer parameters are updated together with the head.

The paper also keeps language modeling as an auxiliary objective during fine-tuning. I find this detail especially important: while the model specializes on a small dataset, it continues to be rewarded for the behavior learned during pre-training. The final loss combines the supervised task with next-token prediction, reducing the risk that specialization will quickly erase too much of the model’s general language ability.

### Turning tasks into text

GPT-1 still needs to adapt very different tasks to a single causal architecture. The paper does this through input transformations: premise and hypothesis are delimited for entailment; sentence pairs are processed in both orders for similarity; in multiple choice, every candidate answer is concatenated with the context and evaluated separately.

The special `[Extract]` token placed at the end is particularly intuitive. Through causal self-attention it can see everything before it and becomes a summary of the context and the candidate answer. A linear head then uses that representation to assign a score. The model remains the same; what changes is how the problem is serialized and the point from which the answer is read.

### What stays with me

GPT-1 demonstrates that generative pre-training is useful for more than completing sentences. Representations learned through next-token prediction can transfer to very different understanding tasks, producing strong results on nine of the twelve benchmarks studied.

This is not yet a general model that receives a request and simply carries it out. It still needs fine-tuning, labeled data, task-specific input formats, and output heads. But the paper builds the essential bridge: instead of training every system from scratch, we begin with a general language capability. GPT-2 will try to remove part of that adaptation as well.

### Note · page 4

> Image annotation

Per ogni token GPT produce un hidden state:

h_1,h_2,…,h_m

Ma qui interessa soprattutto quello associato al token speciale [Extract] alla fine.

Perché è importante?

Grazie alla causal self-attention, [Extract] può vedere tutto quello che viene prima:

Context + Answer
       ↓
   Transformer
       ↓
h_[Extract]

Quindi Extract diventa una rappresentazione vettoriale che incorpora sia il contesto sia quella specifica risposta candidata.
